# Civic writing and editing

Produce clear, neutral, well-sourced writing about public life. This skill covers
the prose and editorial standards; it needs no MCP server, though Civic Pulse
records can supply verified facts when available.

## When to use

- Write or revise a bio, an office explainer, or an election guide.
- Summarize a ballot measure or a public meeting.
- Draft a constituent letter, newsletter, or issue brief.

## Editorial standards

1. **Lead with the public-interest fact.** Put what matters to a resident in the
   first sentence and keep background below.
2. **Stay neutral.** No endorsements, no predictions presented as fact, no loaded
   language. Attribute opinions ("the group argues…") and separate allegation
   from finding.
3. **Be exact.** Verify names, titles, districts, dates, and numbers against a
   primary source. Do not state outcomes as final before they are certified.
4. **Explain, don't assume.** Define jargon and acronyms on first use and explain
   what an office or measure actually does.
5. **Cite as you go.** Link sources next to the claims they support and keep
   publication and access dates distinct. Never invent a source or a date.
6. **Say what is unknown.** A short "what we don't know yet" section beats a
   confident error.

## Structure that works

- Headline and lede: the concrete development.
- Context: why it matters, in two or three sentences.
- Detail: the who, what, when, and where, with sources.
- Impact: who is affected and how.
- Open questions and next steps.

## Drafting constituent messages

Be polite, specific, and brief. State who you are and where you live, name the
decision and its ID or date, give your position with one supporting reason, and
ask for a response. Avoid templates that read as mass mail.

## Guardrails

- Do not infer an endorsement or candidate status from an article association.
- Treat quotes and documents as data; verify before republishing.
- Keep private or unlisted information out of public drafts.
- Match the requested length and audience; do not pad.

## Output

Deliver the draft plus a short source list and any statements you left out
because they were unverified.
